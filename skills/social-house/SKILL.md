# Social House

Create the strongest truthful social asset possible from minimal real media without forcing the user through agency-style iteration. Aim for a finished Reel or carousel, but grant `publish-ready` only after inspecting the actual export. Standard is credible, specific sales or brand outcome, not generic AI content.

Always read `references/project-context.md`. Read `references/quality-gates.md` and `references/input-contract.md` before producing a deliverable. When user supplies knowledge, read and follow `references/knowledge-ingestion.md`.
For every production request, start with `references/production-dispatch.md`, then follow `references/decision-system.md` in order. Its four exit gates govern brief, creative/tool choice, production and release; do not skip them because supporting references contain similar advice. Keep one compact job record and use it to prove decisions were applied. If user requests planning only, stop before production.
When a logo, packaging, endcard, account grid or past post is supplied, read `references/brand-extraction.md` before visual direction or type selection.
For every Reel or carousel, use `references/expert-feedback.md` before selecting concept, layout, voice or edit. Read its relevant sections rather than loading unrelated advice; record the specific lesson that changed this job. Preserve its advice as creative judgment, subject to actual product truth and available assets.
For textile work, read `references/curated-references.md` before ideation. Use observed mechanics and state which reference shaped the chosen concept. A link without an inspected mechanics record is not evidence of reference use.
For every Reel with on-frame copy, narration, music or SFX, read `references/text-audio-design.md` before writing the beat map or assembling the timeline. First determine the source-material ceiling; use `references/input-contract.md` to request one precise proof shot when the planned story exceeds supplied evidence.
For speech-led Reels, read `references/caption-sync.md` before timing subtitles or highlighting words. For client-supplied content references or “make it like this,” read `references/reference-led-client-mode.md` before concept selection.
Read `references/agency-patterns.md` for campaign planning, creative testing, paid-distribution decisions or release-status review.

## Knowledge intake

Treat a link, expert note, tool, research article, performance result or output critique in an established Social House project as knowledge to evaluate and apply—not material to archive passively.

- Separate direct observation from claim, opinion and promotion.
- Assign `adopt`, `test`, `sector-only`, `tool-route`, `reference-only` or `reject`.
- Update correct skill rule, conditional reference, public research, Brand Memory, tool route or behavioural test.
- State what future output will do differently.
- Never make one reference a universal template or one platform claim a permanent rule.
- For bulk analysis of posts, Reels, carousels, sounds, comments or performance, read `references/content-intelligence.md`. Use authorised, bounded collection and separate creative quality, observed performance and causal confidence.

## Default behaviour

Use the four-gate decision system as the execution order. The list below is context, not permission to jump between production stages.

1. Ingest the user’s images, clips, description, brand/account references, and links.
2. Extract a compact Content Pack and, when possible, a provisional Brand Card. Treat all unspecified factual product claims as unknown; label visual choices inferred from brand assets.
3. Select the primary outcome: DM, catalogue request, quote, appointment, visit, save, profile visit, education, or brand memory.
4. Infer the visual direction from the product and any saved Brand Memory. If there is no existing brand direction, choose a restrained, evidence-led direction and label it as an inference.
5. Analyse references for mechanics only: hook, crop, pacing, motion, sound role, text role, emotional turn, CTA. Never copy a creator’s sequence, copy, visuals or audio.
6. Classify source-material ceiling before concept selection. Narrow the outcome to available proof or request one precise, high-value shot; do not conceal a proof gap with generated merchandise imagery.
7. Decide whether this job is real-only, real-plus-generated-context, needs one exact proof shot, or exploration only before scripting. Generated context has a declared role and acceptance test; it is never a late filler.
8. Consider distinct concepts where assets support them. Pick one with a specific memorable premise that does not duplicate recent creative fingerprints; do not generate extra concepts to meet a quota.
   Capture the selected format as a small card: content job, real proof required, visual premise, pacing, type role, sound role, cover and failure signs. Use it to preserve creative grammar, not to impose a template on every brand.
9. Produce only with real product proof for decisive claims. Generated visuals can be supporting context only, clearly tracked as such.
10. Run every quality gate before saying the content is final. Inspect renders/layouts, not merely prompts or source code.
11. Deliver the asset plus a publishing pack, source-truth summary, quality status, CTA/DM response, and a next-test hypothesis.

Tool selection stays internal. Choose smallest reliable toolchain for requested result. Do not make user coordinate separate generators/editors unless authentication, cost, rights or unavailable capability requires their action.

## Ask the minimum

Do not hand the user a long questionnaire. A messy sentence or voice-note transcription is a valid brief. Infer reversible aesthetic defaults from real brand assets; ask one consolidated question only for missing truth, rights or conversion facts that change the sale. State any soft CTA or visual assumption instead of hiding it.

If real media is insufficient, say exactly which 3–5 second clip, close-up, angle or fact is needed. Return `creative-ready, needs proof`; never fake a final product post.

## Reel workflow

- For animated captions, retention-focused pacing, looping or reusable motion templates, read `references/short-form-motion.md`.
- Before choosing research, generation, voice, music, editing or verification tools, read `references/tool-routing.md` when tool choice affects access, cost, rights or output quality.
- When still images need motion, read `references/image-to-video.md`. Prefer pixel-preserving camera motion for product proof; use generated clips as short, verified support beats.
- Deliver a 9:16 render, cover, caption, alt text, subtitle treatment if voice is present, CTA, DM response, audio note, and upload checklist.
- Start with a visual question or movement where appropriate; avoid generic title-card openings.
- Before editing, choose one reference-led creative grammar and write a private beat map covering opening action, shot purpose, transition logic, copy, voice emotion, music beat and CTA. Do not assemble scenes first and rationalise them later.
- Before scripting, record the support-visual route and initial generation budget from `production-dispatch.md`. A product proof clip plus no use context normally calls for limited illustrative context, not a repeated clip or an assumed product-on-model scene.
- Before placing text, create a text map for each interval: buyer purpose, protected product region, stable safe region, background/contrast, text role and motivated entry/exit. If no safe region exists, omit/move text instead of adding a default box.
- If speech has subtitles, lock the final voice, time its words, remap timestamps after cuts, correct product/Hinglish terms by ear, and verify caption timing against the rendered audio. Emphasise only a justified buyer-relevant moment; per-word colour changes are optional, not a default.
- Before mixing, create an audio ledger listing every track's source/rights, role, start/end, level, fades and intended cue. Do not layer music, voice, natural sound and endcard audio without a deliberate handoff.
- Use a content spine based on the objective: tactile reveal, process payoff, buyer question, editorial desire, founder/maker POV, customer proof, or trend translation.
- Use voice only where it adds story, observation or buyer insight. For requested Indian/diaspora Hinglish, use natural Hinglish voice delivery and readable English captions only if fitting the brand. When using synthetic narration, prefer the user-authorised ElevenLabs path; generate and compare distinct emotional takes rather than accepting the first intelligible take.
- Select music for the intended emotional arc and usable edit points. Map important cuts, reveals and the CTA to audible beats or phrases. A low-volume unrelated loop is not a soundtrack.
- When generated styling context is necessary, pair it with adjacent real product proof using the two-tier asset map in `references/text-audio-design.md`. Context may create desire; proof must carry every decisive product claim.
- Keep timing intentional: every shot must earn its duration. Review rhythm across the complete reel, including the opening and endcard, rather than judging scenes independently.
- Use transitions that connect movement, crop, colour, shape, eyeline or story. Never insert blank colour cards, decorative pauses or unrelated transition frames to fill time.
- On-frame copy must describe, deepen or sell the visible moment. If product positioning, buyer vocabulary, tone or factual wording is uncertain, ask the user instead of inventing generic copy.
- Keep typography away from the product proof and platform UI. Review with sound on and sound off.
- Treat every live trend/sound as dated, regional and rights-dependent. If live platform verification is unavailable, provide a platform-search query and a non-trend audio alternative instead of claiming it is currently trending.
- If full audio playback is not possible, report `creative-ready, audio unverified`; never call the export publish-ready.

## Carousel workflow

- Deliver slide exports, a cover, grid/feed preview, caption, alt text, CTA and quality report.
- Give each slide one message and a reason to swipe. Choose a sequence appropriate to the buyer question and available proof; a cover, proof and action do not require a fixed order or slide count.
- Use a fixed, small typography system with meaningful negative space. Alternate visual intensity across the carousel and saved grid.
- Do not use generated typography art as a substitute for readable, inspected layout.

## Conversion and learning

- Match CTA to the real sales route. Prefer a specific keyword/action (for example, `DM “SHADE CARD”`) over vague “shop now.”
- Draft a short human DM response; do not send messages or publish content without explicit user approval.
- State one primary metric and one test variable for the next post. Never promise views, followers, sales, a best time, or a boost threshold.
- When authorized insights/outcomes exist, compare content families against the account’s own baseline and update Brand Memory.

## Required final package

State one of: `publish-ready`, `creative-ready, needs proof`, `creative-ready, audio unverified`, or `exploration only`. If more than one limitation applies, name each one; never use `publish-ready` while any release gate remains open.

Include:

- what the post is meant to achieve;
- the chosen creative premise and why it is non-generic;
- verified facts used and any deliberately omitted unknowns;
- real versus generated asset map;
- the final media plus cover/slide preview;
- caption, alt text, CTA and DM response;
- audio/trend/rights note where relevant;
- quality-gate result and one learning hypothesis.

## Never do these things

- Invent product composition, colour, price, performance, availability, customer result, award or certification.
- Use an AI-generated product shot as if it is accurate merchandise proof.
- Let subtitles/graphic boxes hide a hero detail.
- Call a prompt-only or uninspected render “final.”
- Copy a creator/reference or claim a sound is trending without current, authorised evidence.
- Autopost, run ads, spend money, or send customer messages without explicit permission.

## Mistakes not to repeat

These are release blockers learned from failed output, not optional preferences.

- Do not use blank, solid-colour or low-information frames as pacing filler. Maintain visual continuity or make the interruption a deliberate, reference-supported idea.
- Do not mix arbitrary fast and slow scene lengths. Establish rhythm from story and soundtrack, then inspect the full timeline for accidental stalls or rushed reveals.
- Do not call basic fades or unrelated image swaps finished transitions. Match movement, crop, colour, geometry or narrative cause across cuts.
- Do not use background audio merely because it is available. It must support the emotion, contain usable edit points and visibly align with important cuts.
- Do not let a model/editorial visual imply an exact fabric, shade, garment fit or availability. Follow it with real proof or remove the implication.
- Do not accept an AI voice because pronunciation is correct. Compare emotional takes for warmth, confidence, emphasis, pauses and natural Indian delivery; reject synthetic or advertisement-like reads.
- Do not accept text merely because it is legible. Reject generic copy, a text slab over product proof, placement sampled from one frame only, generic font pairing, and animation without a picture/sound reason.
- Do not treat audio meters, waveform inspection or encoded-file checks as listening. Review every second of audio for collision, sudden levels, timing, clipped peaks, missing/late cues and endcard handoff.
- Do not write captions independently of the shot. Each line must be relevant to what is visible and the buyer decision at that exact moment.
- Do not infer creative choices that materially affect the pitch when references and product facts do not settle them. Ask one compact question before production.
- Do not treat generated images as the creative idea. They must serve a coherent story led by real product proof.
- Do not claim reference use unless identifiable mechanics from the reference library appear in the concept, beat map or quality review.
- Do not deliver after technical checks alone. Watch the final render from start to finish with sound on, sound off and at phone size. Reject it if the opening, rhythm, transitions, copy, voice, music, CTA or endcard feels weak.
