# Reference library protocol

References are a design-research library, not a copy queue. Store a link, screenshot/asset only when permitted, collector note, collection date, account/creator, sector, format, objective, and a mechanics analysis.

## Mechanics record

For every promoted reference, record:

- first-frame hook and whether it is visual, spoken, written, or sonic;
- product proof: what is actually demonstrated rather than claimed;
- shot/crop progression and visual world;
- pace, motion continuity, transition logic and holds;
- typography, subtitle and cover behaviour;
- sound/voice role and commercial-use caveat;
- caption/CTA path;
- what makes it feel human, distinctive or premium;
- what should **not** be copied;
- a translation hypothesis for the target brand.

## Promotion rules

A reference becomes a reusable pattern only after it has an original translation recipe. Examples:

- “Open a textile Reel with 0.6–1.2 seconds of real macro weave under a moving band of side light; keep copy off that frame.”
- “For a premium carousel, create slide rhythm by alternating close proof, quiet space, full context and information—not by making every slide a text poster.”
- “A trend must pass freshness, commercial eligibility, audience relevance, brand fit and product-truth checks before the system offers it.”

## Reel analysis — 2026-10-04

These notes come from direct inspection of the live Instagram Reels in the seed library. Engagement counts were visible but are not treated as causal evidence. Audio interpretation is limited where Instagram did not expose a track name and the review session was muted.

### Fresh simple Reel — Pratham Digitex

Reference: [Dd6lyDQgRpJ](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dd6lyDQgRpJ/)

- **Observed format:** approximately 10 seconds. Full-frame macro views of the same double-cloth muslin in several colours. Each colour receives its own short tactile shot and a centred shade name such as “MID SAGE,” “BLEACH WHITE,” “CHARCOAL” or “PEACH.”
- **Hook:** the fabric is already close, folded and moving in the first frame. No title card or verbal setup delays the product.
- **Proof:** weave, wrinkles, softness cues, fold behaviour and colour changes are visible at useful scale.
- **Pacing:** one simple repeated visual action per shade. The repetition creates rhythm; colour variation prevents monotony.
- **Typography:** one quiet, uppercase sans-serif label with wide tracking. It identifies the shade without competing with texture.
- **Branding/conversion weakness:** almost no in-video brand memory or buyer action. Caption carries product and wholesale detail.
- **Translation rule:** use real macro footage for every shade; keep each shade label stable and minimal; add a restrained brand signature and final buyer CTA without interrupting the colour rhythm.

### Realism benchmark — Vandana Fabrics

Reference: [DbLXlRcNjcg](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DbLXlRcNjcg/)

- **Observed format:** approximately 8 seconds. Opens on several pastel fabrics draped over a counter, then a person lifts individual pieces toward the camera to reveal pattern, sheen, transparency and scale.
- **Hook:** the viewer sees the complete colour family immediately, then receives closer proof.
- **Proof:** a real hand, real shop shelves and fabric held under ordinary store light make the product believable. The background establishes inventory and business context without explanation.
- **Pacing:** short, direct reveal sequence. Cuts follow the same physical action, so transitions feel continuous even when colours change.
- **Typography:** consistent two-line lockup remains over the lower-middle region. The serif/script combination feels traditional-luxury, although the script is less readable at phone size.
- **Sound:** “Golden Days” is shown as the attached audio. Its role appears atmospheric rather than explanatory.
- **Conversion:** caption connects texture, sheen and colour to an in-store visit; no aggressive on-screen CTA.
- **Translation rule:** begin with the full range, then repeat one human handling action across selected shades. Preserve shop reality. Replace decorative script with a more readable brand type system.

### Atelier / craft world — Aso Adire

Reference: [DcTw2-NNQKp](https://www.instagram.com/reels/DcTw2-NNQKp/)

- **Observed format:** approximately 7 seconds. High-contrast monochrome macro of a sewing machine, first poised over the work and then actively stitching patterned cloth.
- **Hook:** machinery fills the frame before any explanation. The unresolved question—what is being made?—creates attention.
- **Proof:** real needle, thread, foot and textile movement communicate work and craft more strongly than quality adjectives.
- **Pacing:** deliberately slow opening hold followed by one satisfying action payoff. Few shots, strong tension.
- **Typography:** no text over the hero process. “COMING SOON” and website stay in Instagram caption area rather than covering the work.
- **Branding:** mood and craft are memorable, but the product offer remains intentionally unrevealed.
- **Translation rule:** use a close mechanical or hand action as a visual question; reveal material contact as payoff; reserve this structure for craft, process or launch anticipation rather than direct catalogue selling.

### Texture macro hook — Mark & Peanni

Reference: [Dd8I3ObNQmO](https://www.instagram.com/reels/Dd8I3ObNQmO/)

- **Observed format:** approximately 17 seconds. A sequence of neutral fabrics shown through hand interaction, stacked folds, selvedge detail, extreme weave macro and sculpted drape, ending on a restrained branded lockup.
- **Hook:** fingers separate fabric layers in extreme close-up, immediately implying touch, weight and construction.
- **Proof:** the Reel varies evidence: hand feel, colour family, surface structure, branded selvedge and drape. It does not rely on one repeated swatch shot.
- **Pacing:** calm but continuously progressive. Each shot answers a different material question; the branded endcard arrives only after proof is established.
- **Typography:** almost no on-frame copy. Product visuals carry the message. Final logo sits over a softly blurred continuation of the material world, avoiding a disconnected blank card.
- **Conversion:** caption uses a short brand thought—“The fabric behind every well-made look”—and a soft discovery CTA.
- **Translation rule:** plan a proof ladder: touch → range → construction → identity → drape → brand. Keep the endcard visually connected to the preceding fabric rather than cutting to a flat screen.

## Cross-reference pattern map

### Patterns shared by the strongest Reels

1. **Product action begins immediately.** Fabric is moving, handled, folded or being worked in the first frame. None opens with a standalone title card.
2. **Texture replaces adjectives.** Close weave, folds, sheen, needle movement and hand interaction prove qualities that generic copy would only claim.
3. **One visual grammar governs each Reel.** Shade-by-shade macro, repeated hand reveal, monochrome craft action or a progressive proof ladder. Formats do not mix unrelated scene styles.
4. **Real environments increase trust.** Shop shelves, hands, machines, work surfaces and imperfect physical details make the material credible.
5. **Text is subordinate.** Best examples use no text or one stable typographic role. Text labels what is visible; it does not narrate an unrelated sales script.
6. **Variation happens inside continuity.** Colour, crop or proof type changes while lighting, action, movement direction and framing remain related.
7. **Branding follows desire.** Brand marks appear subtly during the Reel or after product proof. A connected endcard performs better aesthetically than an abrupt solid-colour interruption.
8. **Short does not mean rushed.** Reels range from roughly 7 to 17 seconds, but each uses only as many shots as the central idea needs.

### Four reusable Reel structures

- **Colour rhythm:** real macro movement repeated across shades; label each shade; end with range CTA.
- **Human reveal:** establish full range; repeat one hand action across hero variants; close with buyer route.
- **Process tension:** show tool or hand before result; hold anticipation; reveal material transformation; use for craft or launches.
- **Proof ladder:** touch → colour range → construction → identity detail → drape/use → visually connected endcard.

### Rules for Social House production

- Choose one structure before scripting or generating visuals.
- Build a shot list around real proof; generated imagery may show styling context only.
- Every cut must either continue an action or introduce a new proof dimension.
- Never insert a blank colour frame between product shots.
- Keep on-screen copy to one job: shade label, product identifier, buyer fact or CTA.
- For a wholesale selling Reel, add conversion without destroying restraint: show the range early, verify product details through real footage, then use one explicit final action such as `DM “SHADE CARD”`.
- Preserve visual continuity into the endcard by using product colour, texture, motion or background from the preceding shot.
- Select music after the structure is chosen, then map reveal points and cuts to audible beats. Do not add a generic music bed after editing.

## Anti-copy rule

Never lift a creator’s complete sequence, distinctive visuals, voiceover, caption, audio, edit, or individual asset. Translate the underlying communication mechanism into the target brand’s actual product, visual world, facts and audience need.
