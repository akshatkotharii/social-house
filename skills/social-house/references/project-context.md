# Project context and north star

## Why Social House exists

Many Tier 2 and Tier 3 businesses and MSMEs have strong products, operating experience and local trust, but their brand recognition remains limited to existing markets. Social media can help them reach new customers and geographies with relatively low distribution cost.

The obstacle is sustained execution. Agencies cost money, require management and rarely produce immediate results. Social growth compounds slowly, so businesses often stop before learning what works. This problem comes from direct experience with a family textile business.

## Product thesis

Give a business one capable content operator through a skill. User supplies minimal real footage, product facts and optional references. Skill handles research, concept, tool selection, production, voice, captions, cover, conversion path and quality control.

User should not need to learn or coordinate every image, video, audio and editing tool. Tool complexity belongs behind skill.

## Quality ambition

Output should express genuine belief in product. Voice may sound confident, enthusiastic, intimate or persuasive because speaker understands and likes what is being sold—not because script adds exaggerated adjectives.

Generated models, environments, product-use concepts and support shots are allowed when useful. They must remain visually coherent and must not falsify product-specific colour, material, motif, construction, fit or performance.

Goal is not automatic content volume. Goal is consistent, distinctive content that reaches relevant people, strengthens brand memory and creates useful actions such as qualified DMs, catalogue requests, dealer enquiries, visits or sales.

## Current test bed and future scope

Begin with textile businesses because source knowledge and real use cases exist there. Architecture must remain sector-independent. Textile-specific rules belong in a sector reference, never universal core.

## Continuous learning commitment

Founder will keep supplying reference posts, Reels, research articles, expert feedback, tool discoveries and output critiques. Each input must be evaluated and translated into a decision, rule, test, tool route or documented rejection. Do not merely archive or summarize it.
