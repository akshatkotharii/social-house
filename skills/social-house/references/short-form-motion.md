# Short-form motion and caption rules

Read this reference when a Reel needs animated captions, retention-focused pacing, looping, programmatic motion or a reusable video template.

## Adopted principles

- Put the strongest truthful visual in the first frame. Do not fade up from black or spend the opening on a title card unless the concept specifically depends on that interruption.
- Keep one memorable premise per short. Every scene must either advance that premise, add proof, create anticipation or deliver the action.
- Build animation and edit timing from the render timeline, not wall-clock timers. Outputs must be deterministic and seekable.
- Inspect at least the opening, a representative middle beat and the ending in addition to watching the complete render.
- Treat safe areas as a preview requirement, not a permanent pixel guarantee. Use a conservative 9:16 working box, then verify against the current platform UI and cover crop.
- Use loops only when they support the objective. A seamless loop can help a tactile or curiosity Reel; it can weaken a wholesale DM Reel if it removes the CTA or brand close.

## Caption timing

Distinguish two different text systems:

- **Subtitles:** timed transcription of spoken audio. Derive word or phrase timing from the final voice track. Never divide a sentence evenly across its duration. Re-transcribe after changing the voiceover.
- **Designed copy:** hook, product label, proof point or CTA. Time it to the visual beat and reading duration, not to unrelated spoken words.

For subtitles:

- Prefer short phrase pages rather than walls of text.
- Use one restrained active-word treatment only when it fits the brand. Word-by-word popping is useful for energetic educational or creator-led content, but usually wrong for quiet premium product films.
- Keep type readable over changing footage with contrast, outline, shadow or a carefully placed scrim. Do not cover product proof.
- Produce a plain subtitle file when useful for accessibility or reuse, even when the social export has burned-in subtitles.

## Pacing and pattern interrupts

Do not impose a fixed cut interval. Choose pacing from the product, brand, audio and idea.

- Energetic educational or talking-head content may need frequent caption, crop, angle or b-roll changes.
- Premium tactile product content may hold longer when material movement, changing light or human interaction sustains attention inside the shot.
- An interrupt must reveal new information or renew attention. Random punch-ins, zooms and hard cuts are not value.
- Diagnose pacing from actual retention data when available. Do not claim universal thresholds or algorithmic distribution cut-offs.

## Verification

Before delivery:

1. Inspect opening, middle and final frames with real shipped assets and copy.
2. Confirm resolution, frame rate, codec, duration and audio presence from the encoded file.
3. Watch once with sound, once muted and once at phone size.
4. Check subtitle timing against the final audio, not the draft script.
5. Check first-to-last continuity when using a loop.
6. Reject any output with a weak first frame, accidental dead frame, unsafe text, timing drift or an ending that undermines the CTA.
