# Selective agency-pattern reference

Source reviewed: [msitarzewski/agency-agents](https://github.com/msitarzewski/agency-agents), 2026-10-08. This is a large catalogue of role prompts, not a Social House dependency or an instruction to imitate an agency workflow.

Use this reference when planning a campaign, testing a conversion creative, considering paid distribution, or deciding whether an asset is genuinely ready to release.

## Adopted patterns

### 1. Give every asset one job

Before production, state one content role: `desire`, `proof`, `education`, `conversion`, `trust`, or `community`. A post may support other outcomes, but it must have one primary job and one thing the viewer should remember.

Write a compact creative hypothesis:

```text
Buyer: [specific buyer and context]
Barrier or desire: [one tension this creative resolves]
Promise: [one truthful, memorable idea]
Proof: [named real shots, facts or supplied assets]
Action: [one response route]
Test variable: [the one thing that may change next time]
Success signal: [qualified DM, shade-card request, save, etc.]
```

This is a planning device, not an excuse to force every Reel into a hook-body-CTA formula.

### 2. Build the viewer journey before choosing effects

Each Reel beat or carousel slide must earn its place: create desire, establish proof, answer a buyer question, reveal a choice, or make the action easier. Choose cut points, type, sound and transition logic after that journey is clear. Keep the product and the story ahead of editing novelty.

### 3. Treat paid distribution as an audit, not a reward

Before a boost, document objective, audience, geography, budget, response capacity, offer truth, conversion route and measurement window. The creative needs a clear message match with the profile, DM reply, catalogue or landing destination. Record what changed before interpreting results.

### 4. Make release status evidence-based

Default to `needs work` until the actual export has passed all relevant visual, audio, truth, rights and conversion checks. A good prompt, source file, agent report or technical render is not proof that the content works. A first pass normally needs review; use the status labels in `quality-gates.md` rather than a score invented from confidence.

### 5. Keep research honest

For any advice derived from platform research, distinguish direct observation, performance association and causal claim. Retain source, date, scope and limitation. Repeated blog claims are not independent evidence.

## Explicitly rejected patterns

- Mandatory slide counts, fixed carousel funnels, fixed hook durations or universal posting/retention thresholds.
- “Viral” or conversion-lift promises without matched account evidence.
- Autonomous publishing, scheduling, spending, messaging, or music selection.
- Generic persona output that could fit a different brand after a logo swap.
- Importing platform-specific claims from YouTube, Google Ads or other channels as Instagram/Facebook rules.
- Treating multiple sources that repeat the same claim as independent proof.

## Adaptation for Social House

For a textile wholesaler, the promise may be a useful selection cue, while proof stays real: weave, shade range, selvedge, hand interaction, drape, buyer use case or a verified process detail. For other sectors, replace textile proof with the product's honest equivalent. Generated material may support mood or explanation but cannot become decisive proof.
