# Social House decision system

Use for every production request. Keep one compact job record; do not expose a long form to the user. A gate fails when its evidence is missing, even if the output looks attractive. Do not render first and invent the reasoning afterward.

## Gate 1 — Understand the job and evidence

Read the complete request, including corrections and attached media. Inspect actual assets, not filenames. Complete `production-dispatch.md` first. Record:

- Deliverables and format: Reel length/orientation, carousel, cover, caption, endcard, language, platform.
- Business: exact product, buyer, region, brand, intended action and response route.
- Fact ledger: user-confirmed facts, facts visibly proved by real assets, unknowns, prohibited claims.
- Asset ledger: real proof, real support, generated context, logo, supplied audio/endcard, rights, dimensions, usable moments, defects.
- Brand Card: known identity or a labelled provisional card extracted from logo, packaging, endcard or past work. Check recent content if supplied.
- Source-material ceiling: what story can honestly be told from these assets?

Make an acceptance checklist from the user's words. Include specific corrections from prior attempts. Separate **required**, **optional** and **not authorised**. Never silently substitute “attractive” for the actual goal, such as qualified DMs.

Ask at most one compact batch of questions when missing information changes product truth, sales promise, response route or rights. Do not ask for an aesthetic preference that a visible brand asset can answer. For a logo with a white background, inspect whether it is an intentional lockup; remove the background only if the artwork can be preserved cleanly and its use is permitted. If missing evidence cannot be safely inferred, offer one exact proof shot or a narrower concept. Proceed on reversible aesthetic choices with explicit assumptions.

**Exit:** brief, fact ledger, asset ledger, acceptance checklist and ceiling exist. Otherwise stop concept work.

## Gate 2 — Decide creative and tool route

Use expert-feedback sections relevant to the current format before ideation: always brand feel, proof/texture, typography and negative space; then Reel/carousel-specific sections. For textiles, inspect the curated reference mechanics. Pull only relevant sections of other resources: text/audio, image-to-video, caption sync, tool routing, and format playbook. Record **which specific lesson changes this job**; a generic “used references” statement fails.

Choose one content job, buyer tension and memorable premise. Compare a small set of genuinely different approaches only if needed; select one against evidence, brand feel, conversion fit, available assets and cost. Write a short creative contract:

- Hook or first-frame action; proof progression; one payoff; buyer action.
- Real proof in each decisive moment; generated context and its limits.
- Reference mechanic translated originally, not copied; deliberate departure from recent outputs.
- Reel: audio role, visual rhythm, text role, cover and endcard handoff. Carousel: slide argument, visual rhythm, cover and grid role.
- Failure signs: what would make this look generic, false or boring?

Select and record the support-visual route: real-only, real-plus-generated-context, request-one-shot or exploration-only. Plan each needed shot. Prefer real footage and crops first. Generate only missing **support** with an explicit purpose and acceptance test; do not depict invented product colour, weave, garment fit or stock as proof. A model image is styling illustration unless a real product-on-model source verifies it. Check actual provider access, licence, watermark, output constraints and cost before generation. Record expected spend/credits and a stop limit. Start within `production-dispatch.md` initial budget; no mass variants or live trend hunt unless it can change the creative choice. A current sound needs dated, region/account-relevant evidence and commercial eligibility; otherwise make a clean master with a licensed/original option.

Route by need, not by tool collection:

- Product-accurate motion: real clip or pixel-preserving HyperFrames/FFmpeg move. AI image-to-video only for a necessary support beat after `image-to-video.md` checks.
- Missing illustrative setting/model: approved image generator, then compare palette and plausibility. Never ask generator to invent exact merchandise; use a user-supplied garment reference if exact fit matters.
- Voice: real approved recording first if available; commercially licensed ElevenLabs for synthetic Hinglish when voice earns its place. No forced voice on a visual-first idea.
- Sound: owned/licensed or Meta-eligible music for commercial use; platform trend audio only after live account-level check. Original/natural sound can be stronger.
- Assembly, typography and export: HyperFrames when precise timed layout matters; ChatCut when connected timeline/generator workflow is faster. Both require final export review.

For each generation, write a *shot prompt*, not a mood adjective dump: subject and source reference, permitted motion/action, camera/framing, light/location, duration, aspect, product details that must stay fixed, prohibited hallucinations and the exact pass/fail check. For voice, write performance direction separately from script: listener, intent, emotional turn, stressed words, pauses, pace and pronunciation. Generate a low-cost sample; reject or revise against the pass/fail check before making variants. `tool-routing.md` owns current access, rights and fallback details.

**Exit:** creative contract, support-visual route, shot plan, tool route, cost ceiling and proof boundaries exist. Otherwise do not produce.

If concept choice is genuinely open, prepare up to three differentiated treatment cards and mark one recommendation. Do not produce three full variants by default. Show the voice/music/language direction and whether model/location imagery is generated; ask one compact question only if that choice materially changes truth, brand representation, licensing or cost.

## Gate 3 — Produce in dependency order

For a spoken Reel, settle the message before editing. Write a short, product-specific voice script; mark pronunciation, emotional intention, emphasis and pauses. Test delivery against buyer, not “AI voice quality.” When ElevenLabs is available and commercially licensed, compare at least two purposeful takes; prefer a real approved voice or a no-voice concept over an unconvincing read. Select music/sound for the emotional arc and usable cut points, not trend status alone. Map all tracks, including supplied endcard audio, in an audio ledger before assembly. Choose which track owns each second and how handoffs work.

Then build visual beat map to actual audio and proof. Each shot needs a buyer-facing job, duration and motivated cut. Generated still/video must match palette and surrounding footage; compare product-related frames with source. Never repeat one clip simply to fill time. Keep the existing endcard only if its duration, audio and visual handoff suit the piece; trim or request approval for alteration when branding would otherwise break.

Write on-frame copy **after** seeing shot composition. Text is optional. For each line: exact claim/purpose, time interval, protected product area, stable negative space, contrast through the whole interval, font role/size/colour, entry/exit cue. Use owned brand fonts if available; otherwise a restrained temporary system, normally no more than two families. No default boxes, generic adjectives, floating captions or animated type competing with fabric. Match captions to final speech after cuts; check product/Hinglish words manually. Design cover independently and inspect at grid size. For a carousel, design the visual proof and slide-to-slide story first, then add only information that earns space; no needless Reel audio step.

Before export, perform a **rough-cut check**: first second, story clarity, every shot's purpose, pace, real/generated continuity, text usefulness, sound/visual alignment, CTA timing and whether the buyer has a reason to DM. Fix the concept if weak; do not polish a weak cut.

**Exit:** assembled asset and rough-cut check pass. Otherwise revise within cost ceiling or downgrade status.

## Gate 4 — Adversarial release review

Revisit acceptance checklist and previous failure patterns in `SKILL.md` and `quality-gates.md`. Inspect the actual exported file, not only project code or contact sheets:

1. Watch entire Reel with sound on; hear every second, transitions, voice emotion, music relevance, endcard and final tail. Check technical levels separately.
2. Watch with sound off at phone size; verify visual story, CTA, subtitles, logo and cover.
3. Inspect every text interval across its moving background, not one frame. Check contrast, safe areas, proof occlusion, spelling and timing.
4. Compare generated frames against real product proof; reject colour/weave/stripe/fit drift. Check carousel slides individually and together.
5. Ask: would a relevant buyer understand the product, trust the evidence and know exactly what to message? Would this be recognisable without a logo? Is one moment memorable?

Use a defect log with time/slide, problem, cause, fix and recheck result. Fix the failed interval, then replay the whole export; local fixes can create new defects. Stop after two failed attempts at the same approach: change approach, ask for a specific asset/fact, or report limitation. Never consume credits indefinitely to rescue a weak premise.

Use the single-owner repair protocol in `docs/OUTPUT-OPTIONS-AND-EFFICIENCY.md`: classify first, fix the smallest failed part, rerun deterministic checks, then review the full export once. Do not add a separate model/agent review call by default when local checks settle the defect.

Release status: `publish-ready` only after all applicable checks pass. If playback, account-level sound rights, proof or visual inspection is unavailable, state the exact unverified gate and do not call it ready. Do not publish, spend, boost or send DMs without separate permission.

## Minimal job record

Keep this in a per-job brief or working notes, not another user questionnaire:

```text
User ask / required deliverables:
Buyer / region / desired response:
Confirmed facts / prohibited claims / unknowns:
Assets and proof ceiling:
Provisional Brand Card / evidence / inferred items:
Question asked or assumption made:
Brand feel / selected reference mechanic:
Creative premise / first frame / proof / CTA:
Shot or slide map; real vs generated:
Support-visual route / tool route / rights / estimated spend / stop limit:
Voice, music and endcard plan (Reel only):
Text, font, safe-region and cover plan:
Rough-cut decision:
Final review defects and fixes:
Release status / remaining limits:
```

Do not fill fields with boilerplate. Omit irrelevant fields, but never omit product truth, proof ceiling, tool cost or release status.
