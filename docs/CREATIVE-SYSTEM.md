# Creative system

This system translates the expert feedback gathered for Social House into specific creative decisions. It is deliberately sector-agnostic; textile examples illustrate the rules because that is the current test bed.

## 1. Decide the brand feel before the format

Write a short brand-feel statement before the first Reel or carousel:

> “Quietly premium, tactile and contemporary. It proves craft through light, material and restraint—not heritage clichés or luxury adjectives.”

That statement governs palette, contrast, pace, lens/crop, locations, copy, transition density, music, captions, and cover design. A content system without this layer creates individually attractive but collectively incoherent posts.

## 2. Convert feedback into design rules

### Material / product as hero

Use real evidence: macro texture, folds, light across a surface, hands interacting, drape, reverse side, edge, process, product in use, scale, and before-to-after transformation. For a textile, the viewer should almost feel the weave. For another sector, replace texture with its equivalent proof: food steam and cut, jewellery scale and reflection, a service’s working session, a machine’s result.

### Frame composition

- Use negative space as an intentional visual ingredient, not a vacant place to fill with words.
- Alternate macro, medium, wide, process and human/context coverage.
- Crop asymmetrically when it creates tension or curiosity. Do not centre every product out of habit.
- Keep type away from the product’s proof area and mobile UI zones.
- Reveal selectively: hide → tease → reveal can make a product more desirable than immediate full disclosure.

### Movement and edit logic

- Start with motion already in progress when appropriate: fabric entering, a hand crossing frame, a reveal, an action beginning.
- Match screen direction, eyelines, colour, folds, circular details, or motion energy from one cut to the next.
- Build a transition library during shoots: lens cover, fabric pass, empty frame, turn, pull, flare, wide-to-macro, hand exit/entry.
- Give text and imagery separate motion roles. If the product has beautiful movement, type should stay calm. If the shot is static, typography may take a little more motion.
- A static image with excellent composition can be stronger than a compulsory transition.

### Typography and captions

- Use one main typeface plus one supporting typeface. Premium work often benefits from a serif/sans pairing, but the role matters more than the genre.
- Standardise headline/logo placement for covers, while varying the imagery so the profile grid has rhythm.
- Captions should not cover the key proof. Build subtitles into the brand language; do not default to giant white text in black rectangles.
- First caption line earns the `more` tap. Use line breaks, specific language, natural search terms, and restrained emojis if any.

### Voice and sound

- A voiceover is an emotional performance, not a text-to-speech checkbox. Produce at least intimate, confident, and conversational reads privately; select the one that carries the piece without sounding like an ad.
- If a user chooses a commercial voice provider, use an approved, properly licensed voice; for the current workflow the preferred provider is ElevenLabs.
- A Reel must be reviewed with sound on and sound off. Sound-off viewers need visual comprehension; sound-on viewers need a reason to stay.
- Treat trends as raw material. The business should be recognisably connected to a sound or format, not merely pasted onto it.

### Context, craft and human evidence

- Heritage can look contemporary. Do not add predictable cultural symbols just to signal “traditional.”
- Choose locations because they explain a story: workshop, architecture, a home, a store, a real use setting, natural light—not because they are decorative.
- Show reverse sides, raw material, unfinished work, human hands, and controlled imperfections where they prove authenticity.
- Capture satisfying micro-moments: unrolling, aligning, cutting, folding, stitch pull, packing, transformation. These create natural payoff without forced hooks.

## 3. Build a content house, not a content slot machine

Every shoot should have a coverage checklist:

- 3 macro proof shots
- 3 complete product / outcome shots
- 3 movement shots
- 3 hand or human interaction shots
- 3 process shots
- 3 environment shots
- 3 model/customer/context shots where relevant
- 3 transition shots
- 3 BTS/packaging/real-world shots

Record a few seconds before and after an action. Shoot vertical for Reels and Stories, while retaining occasional wider coverage for crop flexibility. Save clean, unbranded originals and organise them by product, colour, collection, campaign, orientation and rights.

## 4. Select a content family instead of repeating one template

The system can choose from these repeatable *structures*, while changing the creative material:

- tactile product reveal;
- process / craft proof;
- transformation or before/after;
- buyer problem to product proof;
- editorial desire / styling;
- product education;
- founder or maker point of view;
- customer evidence / UGC;
- launch anticipation and reveal;
- trend translation, only when it passes relevance, rights and brand checks.

Each structure needs a different objective. A beautiful editorial Reel may be for memory and profile visits; a buyer-guide carousel may be for saves; a proof Reel may be for DMs. Do not force them to optimise for the same number.

## 5. The campaign ladder

Do not spend a collection in a single “new launch” post. A useful sequence is:

`teaser → detail → colour / choice → process → story → reveal → launch → styling / use → customer evidence → FAQ`

After launch, keep extracting proof and stories from the same assets. This is both more credible and more economical than generating a completely unrelated concept every week.

## 6. Feed and cover are separate design surfaces

A Reel cover must be legible and interesting in the profile grid, independent of the Reel itself. Review 9–15 posts together:

- distribute close-up, wide, image-heavy, text-led, quiet and detailed pieces;
- alternate visual intensity;
- retain a consistent headline/logo system without becoming a template wall;
- design the next post relative to the adjacent grid, not in isolation.

## 7. Expert feedback traceability

The detailed feedback that seeded these rules is maintained in the original project’s `SOCIAL_HOUSE_EXPERT_FEEDBACK.md`. It includes brand feel, sensory proof, covers/grids, edit continuity, captions, shoot practice, campaign strategy, community, measurement, and anti-corporate copy guidance. This repository converts those observations into executable gates rather than treating them as a style moodboard.
