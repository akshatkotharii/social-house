# Carousel playbook

## Why a carousel needs its own system

Carousel production is editorial design, not a Reel broken into stills. The reader controls pace, so each slide must earn the next swipe and remain useful when saved or screenshotted.

## Common carousel jobs

- buyer guide / how to choose;
- product or collection reveal;
- material/process education;
- comparison or decision support;
- launch story;
- customer proof / FAQ;
- editorial mood with a soft conversion path.

## Slide narrative

Use 5–8 slides as a practical starting range, then vary by story.

1. **Cover:** one visual question or promise. It must work as a profile-grid tile.
2. **Context:** why this matters to the buyer.
3–5. **Proof:** details, choice criteria, process, comparison, use, or evidence.
6. **Payoff:** resolved choice, reveal, or concise answer.
7. **Action:** a precise next step. This can be a soft ending, not necessarily a loud sales graphic.

Avoid turning each slide into an isolated poster. Use recurring type placement and margins, then create rhythm by alternating close crop, quiet slide, full product, detail, information and human/context frames.

## Design requirements

- Set a type system before designing: main/supporting font, hierarchy, case, weights, spacing, caption placement, and image/text ratio.
- Use intentional empty space. Do not force paragraphs into every slide.
- Write a single message per slide. Reduce prose until it can be understood at feed size.
- Build information into a slide’s composition, never on top of the key visual by default.
- Make the first slide compelling, but ensure subsequent slides reward the swipe with evidence rather than repeating the hook.
- Preview the set as a profile grid and at phone-feed size. A beautiful large artboard can fail at actual viewing scale.

## Text and accessibility

- Use plain, specific language; avoid brochure copy.
- Caption opening should provide an additional hook, question, observation or story rather than restating the cover.
- Include natural category terms for discovery (for example, fabric type or craft technique), but do not write for a search engine at the expense of a human.
- Provide meaningful alt text per slide, particularly where information is visual.

## Asset strategy

Use clean original product images without text/logos alongside detail crops, process, environment and context. Generated images may establish a mood only if clearly separated from actual product claims. A photo-led carousel with a few precise words is usually more credible than a fully AI-generated typography carousel.

## Quality gate

Before export, check:

- Can a viewer understand the premise from the cover alone?
- Is the product/fact shown where it is claimed?
- Does each slide have a clear focal point at small size?
- Are all font styles, margins and colours intentional and consistent?
- Does the final action match the post objective and sales response process?
- Is the set distinct from the last few grid posts?
