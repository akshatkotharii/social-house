# Anti-generic system

Generic AI content is not solved by asking a model to “be more creative.” It is solved by restricting what it can invent, requiring an original point of view, and rejecting outputs that could belong to five unrelated brands.

## The anti-generic contract

Before making anything, create five small artefacts.

1. **Truth map** — every factual product claim, colour, visual detail, person, and result is linked to a real source frame or an explicit user fact.
2. **Reference mechanics sheet** — extract hook, shot order, crop, motion, pacing, sound role, type behaviour, emotional turn, and CTA from references. Do not use creator-specific wording, visuals, or sequence as a template.
3. **Creative premise** — one sentence describing the remembered moment. Example: “The first reveal is a light moving across the actual weave; only then do we show the whole palette.”
4. **Creative fingerprint** — record recent content family, opening device, crop, colour treatment, pace, sound treatment, type placement, CTA and visual signature. Do not repeat the same combination within the recent work window unless it is an intentional series.
5. **Authenticity plan** — label every frame as `real proof`, `real supporting`, `generated context`, or `graphic`. Generated context must never make an unverified product claim.

## Hard bans

- “Premium”, “finest”, “unparalleled”, “timeless”, or “high quality” without visual evidence or a specific verified fact.
- A generated product colour, motif, material, packaging, fit, or detail that differs from the source.
- Random luxury model shots, generic hands, stock office scenes, or visual effects that do not advance the product story.
- Text over the key proof area merely because a caption needs a place to go.
- A trend with no brand/product connection.
- A universal CTA such as “shop now” when the actual business needs a shade-card request, catalogue DM, appointment, quotation, or dealer enquiry.
- Marking an uninspected draft “final.”

## Human-feel tests

Reject or rework a concept when any answer is yes:

- Could the same video/copy sell five unrelated brands with only the logo changed?
- Is the strongest claim an adjective rather than proof?
- Does the product appear late, unclear, hidden, or contradicted by generated imagery?
- Is there no real choice, tension, transformation, craft moment, buyer problem, or reason to care?
- Is every shot, beat, caption and transition moving at the same speed?
- Does the voiceover sound like a brochure rather than a person who understands the buyer?
- Is the CTA disconnected from the objective or from how the sales team actually responds?

## Layout and viewing tests

For Reels, inspect a contact sheet and playback with sound on/off. For carousels, inspect each slide at feed size and the whole set together. Verify:

- headline, subtitles and logo are inside safe zones;
- no text competes with the hero detail;
- contrast and type size are readable on a phone;
- captions are correctly timed and intelligible;
- the cover creates a compelling grid entry;
- the product’s actual colour and details are visible enough to support the CTA;
- any music/audio has a documented source and platform choice path.

## Confidence states

`Publish-ready` means all gates pass and source proof is sufficient.

`Creative-ready, needs proof` means the concept, copy, layout and package are ready but a required real shot/fact is missing.

`Exploration only` means generated or reference-led material can inspire a shoot but cannot be used to represent the actual product.

The system must surface this state prominently. Honest incompleteness is better than polished misinformation.
