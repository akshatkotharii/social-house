# Measurement and DM conversion

## Start with an operating path, not a view target

For a business that sells through Instagram/Facebook DMs, a post should lead somewhere concrete:

`content → profile/DM → keyword or clear question → timely human reply → catalogue/sample/quote/appointment → qualified lead outcome`

If pricing, catalogue, MOQ, service area, response ownership, or turnaround are unclear, more reach may only create lost leads. The content workflow must surface these operational gaps.

## Choose metrics by content job

| Content job | Leading signals | Business signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Desire / editorial | watch time, profile visits, follows | later branded demand / recall |
| Education | saves, shares, useful comments | qualified questions / repeat visits |
| Product conversion | CTA clicks, DMs, keyword use | catalogue requests, quotes, orders |
| Craft / trust | completion, meaningful comments, profile visits | conversion quality over time |
| Community | replies, UGC, conversations | retained customers / referrals |

No individual metric proves sales. Compare content families against the job they were designed to do, and combine platform data with manually tagged lead outcomes whenever possible.

## DM design

- Give every conversion piece one specific action: `DM “STRIPES” for the shade card`, `send “CATALOGUE”`, `book a visit`, or `ask for dealer availability`.
- Match the keyword to the asset so attribution is understandable.
- Prepare a short, human reply template and a named owner. The system may draft it, but should not send messages without explicit authorisation.
- Tag incoming enquiries by campaign, product, lead type and outcome where the business can do so responsibly.

## Learning loop

1. Define a hypothesis: “Macro proof + shade-card CTA will increase qualified DMs compared with a full swatch opening.”
2. Publish or test under normal account conditions.
3. Import insights via authorised account access/export or a manual log.
4. Compare against that account’s own recent baseline and against the intended content family.
5. Change one variable next: opening visual, hook, length, CTA, cover, sound treatment, or audience—not all at once.
6. Record the result in Brand Memory and update the creative fingerprint.

Do not claim a universal best posting time or boost threshold. Build an account-specific baseline after a meaningful sample; audience behaviour, offer, geography, season and account history differ.

## Paid boost guardrails

Boosting is not a default output. It should be considered only when:

- the campaign has a defined business objective, audience, geography, budget and measurement window;
- the organic post already demonstrates the intended quality signal for its job, relative to the account’s baseline;
- the creative and landing/DM path are ready to handle response;
- the account owner explicitly approves paid spend and has the required Meta access.

After a boost, record objective, audience, creative, spend, delivery, click/DM/lead quality and outcome. The next decision is based on the business result, not merely low-cost impressions.

## Community as a research engine

Save strong comments and recurring DMs. Repeated questions are a content backlog: material care, availability, fit, technique, styling, selection, delivery, or pricing process. Give UGC a useful prompt rather than a vague `tag us`: unboxing, styling, texture close-up, before/after, or customer story. Seek permission before reposting.
